# An original story example: Why a conveyor needs a buffer

This is an original planning example, not the narration of either supplied video and not the 12-second rendering demo. It shows how to transfer the same visual grammar away from houses and driveways. Treat the factory as a simplified illustration.

## Narration draft

Put two machines on a conveyor, and it looks like one smooth line. The first machine fills a box. The second closes it. Simple.

But the closer sometimes pauses. The filler keeps sending boxes, and the belt runs out of room. Now one tiny delay has stopped the whole line.

So you add a short waiting lane between them. When the closer pauses, boxes move into the empty spaces. This is a buffer.

It buys time, but it cannot create unlimited space. Leave the closer stopped for long enough, and the waiting lane fills too.

So a sensor watches the last free position. Before another box reaches the crowded lane, it tells the filler to wait. When the closer starts again, the queue begins to shrink.

Now the sensor releases the next box only when there is room. The machines can run at different moments without pushing into each other.

The extra conveyor did not make the slow machine faster. It gave a short delay somewhere to go.

## Visual transfer

| Beat | Visual | Style preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Establish | Small 3D factory station on a cutaway floor; warm off-white machines, gray belt, green status accents | Miniature world, high three-quarter composition |
| First failure | Rigid cardboard boxes accumulate at a stopped closer | One physical cause, one visible consequence |
| Buffer reveal | Camera moves to plan; extra lane slides/reveals, numbered spaces light briefly | Motivated reframe and mechanism overlay |
| Remaining limit | Queue fills the lane; one red/white status stamp | Controlled failure graphic |
| Better mechanism | Close insert shows sensor, wire/path and a gate/stop | Label/leader and exposed mechanism |
| Test | Pause and resume the closer; queue drains coherently | Smooth deterministic 30 fps object motion |
| Payoff | Return to wide view; boxes move without collisions | Familiar scene anchor and resolved causal chain |

Create new shapes and industrial props, not a driveway with its labels renamed. Keep the rendering palette/material hierarchy, typography and camera grammar. Do not introduce a lip-synced character, global cartoon ink or a dramatic trailer score by default.
